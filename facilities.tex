\section*{Facilities, Equipment, and Other Resources}

\vspace{0.2em}
\subsection*{University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP)}
MD Armanuzzaman is an assistant professor in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP). The Department of Computer Science has strong research and educational programs in cybersecurity and AI, including a cybersecurity specialization in the M.S. in Software Engineering and dedicated B.S./M.S. programs in AI. UTEP is an R1 research university and is designated as a National Center of Academic Excellence in Cyber Defense and Cyber Operations by the NSA and DHS. These institutional strengths are highly aligned with the proposed work on secure embedded systems, hardware security, and trustworthy computing. The department also maintains a strong culture of research mentorship and interdisciplinary collaboration, which supports the proposed project and its student development activities. The PI's faculty office is in the Chemistry and Computer Science Building.

\vspace{0.25em}
\subsection*{T\textsubscript{3}SLab at UTEP}
The T\textsubscript{3}SLab: Trusted Systems \& Software Security Lab, led by MD Armanuzzaman, supports research in embedded systems security, systems and software security, hardware security, FPGA security, and agentic systems security. The lab currently supports one Ph.D. student, two M.S. students, and two B.S. students. It includes high-speed wired and wireless connectivity, dedicated workstations, and a suite of embedded security evaluation tools, including the Renesas RA8M1 Cortex-M85 evaluation board, Cortex-M33 development boards, the ARM Versatile Express V2M-MPS3 prototyping board, a Dell PowerEdge R570 server with an NVIDIA L4 PCIe GPU (72W, 24 GB, passive, single-wide full-height), and the NewAE ChipWhisperer HuskyPlus kit. These resources are used for microcontroller security analysis, side-channel evaluation, firmware attestation, hardware-enabled trust, FPGA-based experimentation, and secure embedded-system design. The lab also maintains standard desktop workstations and software development tools for implementation, debugging, and evaluation.

\vspace{0.25em}
\subsection*{High Performance Computing (HPC) Resources}
UTEP maintains centralized HPC resources for large-scale analysis and AI/ML workloads, with support from professional staff and integration with the Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC) at UT Austin. The campus operates three clusters: JAKAR (2,880 cores, 28 TB RAM), PARO (913 cores, 11.5 TB RAM), and PUNAKHA, UTEP's GPU-accelerated AI cluster. PUNAKHA includes two NVIDIA DGX/HGX nodes with twelve NVIDIA H100 GPUs, 3 TB system memory, 30 TB local storage, and NDR200/200GbE InfiniBand connectivity. It runs Red Hat Enterprise Linux 9.2 with OpenHPC 3 and uses Docker and SLURM for job scheduling and MIG-based GPU allocation. These systems provide the computational capacity needed for large-scale modeling, benchmarking, and AI-assisted security analysis.
