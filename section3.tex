\section{System and Threat Model}
\label{sec:system_threat}

We consider embedded systems based on FPGA SoC platforms and Arm Cortex-M microcontroller units (MCUs).
FPGA SoCs provide programmable logic composed of configurable logic blocks, Adaptive Logic Modules (ALMs), lookup tables (LUTs), flip-flops, and related resources that can be interconnected to construct customized hardware modules or soft-core CPUs.
Cortex-M MCU systems may execute bare-metal firmware or an RTOS with multiple tasks, interrupt handlers, trusted services, and peripheral-facing components.
We assume the availability of hardware security mechanisms such as secure boot, a TEE, memory protection, and hardware cryptographic support.
A remote verifier is assumed to maintain the expected hardware/software configuration and verify evidence generated by the embedded device.

Our threat model evolves with the \sys~trust lifecycle.
In the first thrust, during trust establishment, an adversary may attempt to cause an unauthorized hardware or software configuration to be accepted as trusted.
In the second thrust, during runtime execution, the adversary may exploit memory-corruption vulnerabilities in applications, libraries, drivers, RTOS components, or interrupt handlers to hijack control flow, corrupt measurement state, or interfere with attestation through interrupts and task transitions.
In the third thrust, during containment and recovery, a compromised component may attempt to cross compartment boundaries, reuse authenticated state across compartments, or access unauthorized memory and peripherals.
We assume that the root of trust and immutable trusted components are correctly provisioned, standard cryptographic primitives remain secure, and protected hardware key registers cannot be modified by unauthorized software.
Physical invasive attacks and destructive denial-of-service attacks are outside the scope of this project.
