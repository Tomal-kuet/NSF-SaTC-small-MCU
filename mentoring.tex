\section*{Mentoring Plan}
\label{sec:mentoring}

\textbf{Overview and Philosophy.} MD Armanuzzaman is committed to mentoring students in a way that combines rigorous research training, professional development, and inclusive support. My mentoring philosophy is informed by my experience in embedded systems security, trusted execution environments, FPGA-based SoC security, and hands-on cybersecurity training. I aim to prepare students for careers in academia, industry, and public-sector research by helping them develop technical depth, research independence, and strong scientific communication skills.

\textbf{Orientation and Goal Setting.} Each student will begin with an Individual Development Plan (IDP) that identifies research interests, skills, milestones, and career goals. The IDP will be reviewed regularly with the PI to track progress, clarify expectations, and adjust goals as needed. Students will meet regularly for one-on-one mentoring and project discussions, with clear expectations for research conduct, documentation, collaboration, and professional development.

\textbf{Research Training and Scholarly Development.} Students will be integrated into the project through research tasks aligned with their background and career goals. Graduate students will work on embedded systems security, control-flow attestation, trusted execution environments, hardware security, and FPGA/SoC security, while undergraduate and master's students will contribute to focused technical tasks, prototyping, evaluation, and documentation. Mentees will learn how to formulate research questions, review prior work, and write technical reports. They will also receive guidance on manuscript preparation for venues such as IEEE S\&P, ACM CCS, USENIX Security, NDSS, IEEE/ACM EMSOFT, ASIACCS, TDSC, and TIFS.

\textbf{Hands-on Research and Professional Communication.} Students will participate in weekly group meetings, technical presentations, and project reviews, where they will present progress, challenges, and ideas. This helps them develop technical confidence, oral communication, and the ability to explain complex systems clearly to diverse audiences. I will coach students on experimental design, figure preparation, manuscript writing, and response to reviewer feedback so they can grow into independent researchers.

\textbf{Career Development and Networking.} Graduate students will gain experience in proposal writing and professional networking. They will contribute to problem formulation, technical planning, and drafting of project materials while receiving career advice on academic, industry, and government pathways. Students will be encouraged to attend workshops, present at conferences, and engage with collaborators and external researchers.

\textbf{Effective Collaboration.} The project will leverage collaborations with faculty and research groups from Northeastern University, the Rochester Institute of Technology, and the University of Colorado Colorado Springs (UCCS). These collaborations provide students with access to complementary expertise in embedded systems, software security, hardware security, and applied cybersecurity research. Students will participate in regular cross-institutional meetings and technical exchanges to build strong collaborative habits and gain exposure to diverse perspectives in systems security research.

\textbf{Peer Mentoring and Professional Integrity.} Students will mentor junior researchers and contribute to a collaborative research culture. Senior students will guide younger students in literature review, implementation tasks, debugging, and technical presentation, while all mentees will be trained in responsible conduct of research, authorship, data handling, reproducibility, and ethical dissemination of results. Progress will be assessed through IDPs and regular feedback sessions.

\textbf{Expected Outcomes.} This mentoring plan will prepare students to conduct high-quality systems security research, produce scholarly work, present at conferences, and contribute to a broader research community. By the end of the project, students are expected to have co-authored publications, developed technical artifacts, and built the professional foundation needed for successful careers in research and engineering.
